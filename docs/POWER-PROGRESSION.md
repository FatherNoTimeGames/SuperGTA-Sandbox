# Power progression

[Documentation index](README.md)

## Confirmed

- Supers have human-scale physical capabilities by default. Specific powers create exceptions. Direct street-to-rooftop shortcuts distinguish powers that enable them and preserve the value of ordinary routes.
- Starting powers should feel like Witcher Signs in scale and immediate utility. A short fire blast, a brief speed burst, and a jump onto a one-story roof illustrate the intended scale.
- Human attributes and ordinary skills improve through practice independently of power points, including on a build fully invested in a capstone. They improve the performance of existing actions through numeric bonuses.
- Sandbox actions feed one shared character XP track, including actions that also train individual skills. Each character level grants one power point.
- The opening starts with human-scale sandbox play. The first level-up earns the first power point through ordinary sandbox XP, so players learn what their character and equipment can do before adding a power.
- Keep power points limited. Each purchase should change how a build approaches situations and compete with other worthwhile purchases. XP pacing, purchase costs, and the total budget are open.
- Keep guns and cars useful across most of progression. Evaluate powers by how they change when a player wants to shoot, drive, or use an ordinary route.
- The speedster capstone is a manual toggle. While active, it slows the surrounding world and preserves the familiar pace and handling of the player's existing superspeed. The effect stays active when the player stops moving and lasts until toggled off, with unlimited duration and no resource drain.

## Provisional

- Candidate attribute effects include strength for melee damage, speed for walking and running pace and ordinary dodge or dash recovery, and endurance for stamina. Accuracy or perception could help with weapon spread. Candidate activity skills include melee weapons, martial arts, guns, swimming, and driving. Perception could affect how long an existing focus effect lasts, if supported.
- An attribute or skill threshold can qualify the player to spend power points on a superhuman feat such as lifting a car or dashing. A feat can require several stats when each contributes to what it lets the player do.
- Spending points on a basic special power such as fire or telekinesis could open a separate skill that grows through use and qualifies the player for new abilities. Advanced feats such as sustained flight could combine physical and special-power prerequisites.
- A short, directed push is the preferred starter for telekinesis, with the feel of a Star Wars Force push. Targeting, affected objects and characters, and the strength of their reactions are open.
- Use believable mass and physical constraints to let powers interact with many kinds of objects consistently. The same push can produce different movement depending on mass, friction, where the impulse lands, and whether the object is attached to its surroundings. Free props can use rigid-body simulation. Characters can use the same inputs to resolve reactions while keeping normal movement controllable. A controlled test should compare movable props, characters, vehicles, and anchored objects before setting starter limits.
- Durability could be its own special power. Early benefits should feel plausible at human scale, such as recovering quickly from a hard landing or staying on your feet after a heavy impact. Gunfire would still be dangerous at that stage.
- The strongest abilities could be capstones reserved for players who commit most or all of their power points to one path. Invincibility is a candidate durability capstone. Wide builds would trade access to the highest tier for more ways to solve a problem.

## Open

- What can each starter power do in ordinary sandbox play? Start by deciding how the telekinetic push is aimed and what it affects. Revisit the first-power reveal once the initial choices are defined.
- How should durability handle gunfire on the way to its highest tier? Compare continued vulnerability, situational protection, and passive resistance against the value of guns and cover.
- How exclusive should a capstone be? Compare committing every point to one path with leaving room for a few starter powers while reserving the highest tier for a single path.
- Which numeric bonuses belong to attributes or activity skills? Resolve overlapping candidates such as perception and weapon skill before setting the stat list. Decide whether human focus affects time.
- Decide how the speedster's world slowdown would work in a shared simulation if multiplayer is added.
- How far can one build broaden? The exact roster, skill thresholds, and whether a character can reach the top of several paths are open. Combining top-tier strength, movement, and durability would be a very late-game possibility if supported.
